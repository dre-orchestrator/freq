How Barge Drafting Works Today

Barge draftingThe process of measuring how deep a loaded barge sits in the water to determine cargo weight. — the process of measuring how deep a loaded barge sits in the water to determine cargo weight — is performed manually on virtually every vessel in the US inland waterway system. The process has remained largely unchanged for decades.

A typical manual drafting sequence requires a crew member to walk the barge deck to each draft markMeasurements painted on hull for manual visual reading. location, visually read painted marks on the hull at the waterline, relay readings by radio to a shore-based operator, repeat the process at multiple measurement points along the vessel, and compile all readings into a draft surveyFormal process of calculating cargo weight from draft measurements. report.

This process takes approximately 4 hours per barge, requires crew presence on deck in active operational zones, introduces 1–3% measurement error from visual reading and manual recording, and produces paper-based documentation with limited traceability.
